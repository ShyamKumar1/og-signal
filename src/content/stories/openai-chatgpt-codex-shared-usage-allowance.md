## One allowance across two products

OpenAI's help centre page on ChatGPT and Codex usage limits says that starting in December, the usage allowance on Plus, Pro and Business plans will be shared across ChatGPT and Codex.

OpenAI says the change accompanies bringing the agentic capabilities of Work into the main ChatGPT experience. The two announcements are one announcement. A product merge is also a billing merge.

## Tokens replace messages

Usage will be measured in tokens, the units of information a model processes and generates, rather than in messages. OpenAI says more advanced models and complex tasks generally use more of the allowance.

That is a more honest meter than a message count and a less predictable one. A message count is something a subscriber can estimate. A token count depends on what the model does underneath the message, which the subscriber cannot see.

## Instant chat stays outside the meter

Instant chat will not draw from the shared usage allowance. The carve-out keeps the behaviour most subscribers use for quick questions separate from the metered pool.

OpenAI says it expects most users will not need to wait for their usage to reset more often than they do now. Users will see combined remaining usage and reset times in Settings, under Usage.

## The size of the allowance is unstated

OpenAI's help centre does not state the size of the new shared allowance. Subscribers on the affected plans are being told that the meter is changing without being told what it holds.

That absence is the thing to watch. A shared pool can be generous and still feel smaller, because two products now draw on it, and no published figure lets a user check the arithmetic before the first month lands.

## Codex runs now draw on the chat pool

The practical consequence follows from three stated facts: the pool is shared, it is measured in tokens, and advanced models and complex tasks use more of it. A long Codex session and a day of chat spend from the same number.

That matters most for subscribers who already use both products. Under separate allowances, a heavy Codex week did not touch chat. Under a shared one it does, and there is no published figure to plan around. The help centre describes the mechanism and leaves the size of the pool for later.

What OpenAI does publish is the direction of travel: one subscription, one meter, several products. The company frames it as bringing Work's agentic capabilities into ChatGPT, not as a reduction, and the two claims can both be true.

## A small group goes first

OpenAI says a small group of users may see the usage changes before December, so it can learn from early feedback before expanding more broadly.

TechCrunch reported on 25 November 2026 that OpenAI began rolling out a unified Chat and Work experience to a small group of Pro subscribers. ChatGPT product lead Thibault Sottiaux announced the limited rollout in a post on X on 24 November 2026.

An OpenAI spokesperson told TechCrunch that most users are not expected to face more frequent usage resets, but that a small fraction of the heaviest users may hit their limits sooner. The spokesperson said OpenAI will keep working on efficiency so subscribers get more out of their plans.

## Three options at the limit

At the limit, OpenAI lists three options: wait for the reset, upgrade the plan, or buy credits for eligible features. The third option changes the arithmetic of a subscription, because a ceiling that can be extended by purchase is a ceiling with a price attached.

Anyone budgeting for a team should read the December change that way rather than as a features release. The next thing to watch is whether OpenAI publishes the size of the allowance before it applies to everyone, or only after subscribers have spent a month discovering it.

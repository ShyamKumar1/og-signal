Applied Optoelectronics announced a multiyear long-term agreement with an existing Tier-1 hyperscale datacenter customer on 24 November 2026. The customer has committed to purchase approximately 1.5 billion US dollars of 800G transceivers in 2027 and approximately 0.7 billion US dollars in laser diodes between 2028 and 2029.

The company is based in Sugar Land, Texas, and listed on Nasdaq as AAOI. The buyer is not named.

## The four-year shape

The agreement covers the period from December 2026 through December 2030. Money is committed in three of those four calendar years: 2027 for transceivers, 2028 and 2029 for laser diodes. Other products may be added depending on future needs.

The minimum commitments add up to 2.2 billion US dollars. AOI states them in dollars by product category and does not disclose unit prices or a breakdown of shipment volumes, which means the contract cannot be converted into a count of modules.

## No capital from the buyer

The customer made no commitment of capital as part of the agreement. AOI will contribute a 2 million US dollar one-time expense to accelerate qualification efforts with this customer.

That is a small figure against the contract, and it is the only money AOI says it is putting in. A supply agreement with a minimum purchase commitment is a different instrument from a joint build: the buyer promises to buy, the seller promises to supply, and neither takes equity in the other.

## The $10 billion is a forecast

Stefan Murry, AOI's chief financial officer and chief strategy officer, said the customer's forecast indicates significant demand beyond the purchase commitments over the four-year term. He said that while not guaranteed, the current level of demand based on long-term discussions with the customer exceeds 10 billion US dollars over the four years of the agreement.

Murry also said that expanding production capacity would position AOI to serve that demand, but that additional sales beyond the 2.2 billion US dollars of purchase commitments will depend on bringing that capacity online and securing additional purchase commitments.

Read together, those two sentences put the gap between 2.2 billion and 10 billion on capacity and paperwork rather than on demand. The larger number is what the customer says it might need. The smaller number is what it has agreed to take.

## What an 800G transceiver is

800G transceivers are optical modules that move data between data centre equipment at 800 gigabits a second. AOI describes itself as a provider of advanced optical and high-frequency cable networking products that power AI.

The product sits at the point where compute clusters stop being a collection of machines and become a network. That is why a commitment of this size is reported at all, and why it is reported in dollars rather than units.

## Why the shape matters

A minimum commitment converts a forecast into an obligation on the buyer's side. The obligation is what makes the announcement more than a statement of optimism, and it is bounded: 1.5 billion dollars of transceivers in one year, 0.7 billion dollars of laser diodes across two.

The concentration is the near-term fact. Most of the committed money arrives in a single calendar year, 2027, which puts the delivery risk and the revenue in the same window.

## The disclosure gap

AOI calls the counterparty an existing Tier-1 hyperscale datacenter customer, which is the standard way to describe a large cloud operator without confirming which one. The announcement gives no locations, no volumes and no pricing.

It also does not say what happens if the customer does not meet the minimum, which is the clause that would decide how firm the 2.2 billion actually is.

## What to watch

Two things carry the rest of the story: whether the 2028-2029 laser diode commitments arrive on schedule, and whether AOI announces capacity expansions with dates attached. The company has said the additional sales depend on both, so the next filing is where the 10 billion forecast either acquires a build plan or stays a number in a quote.

Marlowe raised 277 million US dollars in a seed round led by Accel. The company says the round values it at 1.15 billion US dollars, a figure reported by GeekWire. Accel's investment note confirms the size of the round and its lead role, and does not state a valuation.

That split is worth stating plainly. One primary document confirms the money. The number that makes it a unicorn comes from the company, through a news report.

## Founded in May, approaching 30 people

Marlowe was founded in May 2026. It is based in both Bellevue, Washington, and Redwood City, California, and is approaching 30 employees.

Dan Lewis, the chief executive, co-founded the trucking startup Convoy in 2015 and later was an enterprise AI leader and corporate vice president at Microsoft. Kutta Srinivasan, now chief technology officer, was a corporate vice president at Microsoft and the company-wide technical lead for AI infrastructure.

## Inference is not one workload

Marlowe designs and operates computing systems for inference work, built around chips from AMD, Etched and others. It says it plans to select chips and facility designs according to the task, including coding agents and voice assistants, rather than depend exclusively on Nvidia.

The company is not using Nvidia chips for now, but has not ruled them out. That phrasing leaves the door open in a way a pure AMD shop would not need to.

## Two sites, three customers

The first site, built around Etched's chips, is scheduled to go live in December and will primarily serve a large AI coding company. A second, larger site using AMD's Helios systems is on track for the second quarter of 2027.

Both sites are under contract and a third is under a letter of intent. Marlowe says it has signed three customer agreements and is not naming the customers or the locations. A letter of intent is not a contract, so the third site is the least certain part of the build plan.

AMD said in Marlowe's launch post that it is excited to deploy its new Helios system with Marlowe next year.

## The 2-3x claim

Marlowe's founders say in the launch post that even modest gains in network architecture, power oversubscription, AI-driven fleet operations and workload-specific inference optimisation multiply into a 2-3x advantage in performance, availability, resiliency and cost. That is a company claim, made before either contracted site has run a production workload.

The supporting observation in the same post is about construction. Marlowe states that data centre construction costs are up 40 percent since 2020 and that many projects are delayed or cancelled, making every watt more precious.

## Accel's side of the thesis

Accel wrote in its investment note that inference demand is growing faster than power and compute can come online, and that Marlowe is building what it calls the multi-silicon AI factory. The constraint it names is power and compute, not demand.

The note carries the phrase the company now uses for itself. It does not carry the valuation, so the two documents describe the same round at different levels of confidence.

## Who else is in

Alongside Accel, investors include Bessemer, Altimeter, Atreides, Eclipse, Journey, Valor, Scribble, Emergence Capital, Ritual, Pitango, Neo, Village Global and Banter. Angel investors include Box chief executive Aaron Levie, Uber chief executive Dara Khosrowshahi and KKR co-founder Henry Kravis.

## December is the first test

A seed round of this size buys time and hardware commitments, not a result. The December site will run on Etched's chips for a single large AI coding customer, which is the narrowest version of the thesis: one workload, one chip vendor, one customer class.

The second, larger site using AMD's Helios systems in the second quarter of 2027 will say whether the multi-silicon argument holds when the build is bigger and the workloads are mixed. Until then the 2-3x figure is an expectation the company has published about itself.
